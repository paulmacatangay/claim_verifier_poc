A primary key uniquely identifies each tuple in a relation. A relation can have several candidate keys, but it has only one primary key. A foreign key refers to the primary key of another relation and it must always be non-null. Boyce-Codd normal form requires every nontrivial functional dependency to have a superkey on its left side. Isolation means that concurrent transactions behave as if they ran one after another. Atomicity is enforced by the concurrency control system. Two-phase locking guarantees serializability. NoSQL databases scale horizontally by sharding data across nodes.
